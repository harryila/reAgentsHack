VERDICT: The D6 stack is directionally right — grouped CV, paper-level bootstrap, permutation control, min-support thresholds, and a full ranked table are the correct instincts, and the doc already covers pseudoreplication in the CV, duplicate cohorts, and MI-as-descriptive adequately. But the design is under-specified at exactly the points where implementations silently go wrong, and two of the gaps are mechanical defects, not judgment calls: the ΔLL baseline/subset mismatch (inherited verbatim from the §31 pseudo-code) corrupts the core moderator ranking, and unstratified GroupKFold with 3 classes on ~30 papers will crash or poison the overnight M5 run. Beyond those, the three numbers scripted for the 90-second demo — the headline moderator's permutation p (no selection adjustment across 10-20 candidates), the agentic loop's 'held-out gain +X%' (nothing was held out from the agent's selection), and the global entropy (finding-weighted and conditioned on classifiability) — are each dismantlable by one judge question, at an event hosted by a Garden-of-Forking-Paths coauthor. The good news: every fix here is specification, not research — fold-paired within-subset baselines, StratifiedGroupKFold + label smoothing, a two-tier permutation scheme with honest dashes, a free Westfall-Young max-ΔLL adjustment, a paper-split (or relabeled) remap test, paper-level entropy with exclusion-rate disclosure, a post-fit leaf audit with config-pinned dose bins, and two sentences pinning the bootstrap's group-identity and denominator conventions. Half a day of it now, written into D6 before the first line of moderators.py, versus a demo that cannot survive Q&A. Fix findings 1 and 2 before M4; fix 3-5 before the M5 overnight run; 6-8 before demo freeze.

[blocker] (D6 moderator statistics + context §31 reference pseudo-code (rank_moderators)) ΔLL is computed as (baseline log loss on the FULL dataset) minus (moderator log loss on the moderator's NON-MISSING SUBSET). These are losses on different rows with different outcome distributions, so ΔLL confounds moderator signal with missingness-correlated base-rate shifts. The §31 pseudo-code the design inherits has exactly this bug: baseline is fit once on all of df, each feature's loss on df[df[feature].notna()]. A moderator reported only in a homogeneous sub-literature (e.g., dose reported mainly in full-text RCTs that skew null) gets a large fake 'gain' because its subset is intrinsically easier to predict, not because the moderator predicts anything.
FAILURE: Timing has 37% coverage and its subset is 80% null. Subset prior entropy is far below global prior entropy, so ΔLL is large and positive even if timing carries zero information within the subset. Timing headlines the moderator table, drives the tree, and gets narrated in the demo. A judge asks 'is the gain computed against a baseline on the same rows?' — the honest answer is no, and the entire ranking, the centerpiece deliverable, is invalidated on stage. Alternatively nobody asks, and the 'discovered' moderator is a missingness artifact — the science is wrong.
FIX: Recompute the DummyClassifier(prior) baseline inside each moderator's non-missing subset, on the SAME GroupKFold splits, and report fold-paired ΔLL (difference per fold, then mean and sign consistency across folds). One-line change to the loop; also makes ΔLL variance interpretable. Keep the global baseline only as the dashboard's 'overall disagreement' reference, never as the ΔLL minuend.

[blocker] (D6: GroupKFold(k=min(5, n_papers)) with 3-class log loss on ~30 papers) GroupKFold ignores class balance. With ~30 papers, 3 classes, and a rare class (e.g., 'negative' present in 4 papers), folds will routinely lack a class in train or test. DummyClassifier and LogisticRegression then have classes_ missing a label: sklearn log_loss raises ValueError ('y_true contains labels not in labels') unless labels= is passed explicitly; if you pass labels and a fitted model assigns ~0 probability to an unseen true class, a single held-out row contributes ~36 nats (clipped at float eps), swamping the fold mean. ΔLL becomes dominated by fold-composition lottery, and per-moderator subsets make it worse (small strata → more degenerate folds). Nothing in D6 specifies labels handling, smoothing, or stratification.
FAILURE: The overnight M5 run (bootstrap 500 × permutation 200 × moderators × folds) crashes at 2 AM on the first fold missing a class — or worse, doesn't crash and returns rankings where the winner is whichever moderator's subset happened to dodge a 36-nat penalty row. You notice Sunday morning when bootstrap 'stability' is 30% for everything and there's no time to rerun.
FIX: Specify now, in the design: (1) StratifiedGroupKFold (in sklearn since 1.0) instead of GroupKFold; (2) pass the global label set to log_loss everywhere; (3) Laplace-smooth the dummy prior and floor predicted probabilities at a stated epsilon (e.g., 1e-3), documented as a smoothing choice, not silent clipping; (4) assert per-fold class presence and log a warning + skip-with-reason rather than crash; (5) if a moderator's subset has <k papers or a class in <2 papers, report it as 'insufficient for CV' in the ranked table instead of fitting.

[major] (D6 grouped permutation control ('shuffle moderator values at the paper level 200×')) 'Paper-level shuffling' is only well-defined for paper-constant moderators (species, study_type). The money moderators — dose arms, timepoints, outcome family — vary WITHIN papers, and papers contribute different numbers of findings, so 'shuffle at the paper level' has no coherent implementation for them: you cannot hand paper A's 15-value moderator vector to paper B's 2 findings. Whatever the coder improvises decides the p-values: a naive row-level shuffle destroys within-paper correlation, making the null far too easy to beat (anticonservative p on exactly the dose moderator the C-topic story hinges on). The design is also silent on preserving the missingness pattern under permutation — shuffling NaNs changes the analysis subset each iteration, breaking comparability with the observed ΔLL.
FAILURE: Dose gets permutation p=0.005 from a row-shuffled null. A judge — at an event hosted by a Garden-of-Forking-Paths coauthor — asks what the exchangeability unit is and why within-paper dependence was broken under the null but respected in the CV. There is no good answer, and the anti-overfitting control that was supposed to be the credibility centerpiece becomes the credibility hole.
FIX: Two-tier scheme, stated in the doc: (a) paper-constant moderators: permute the paper's single value across papers (within the non-missing paper set), replicate to that paper's findings — valid block permutation; (b) within-paper moderators: do NOT row-shuffle; either collapse to a pre-registered paper-level summary (e.g., max daily dose) and permute that, labeling the test as paper-level, or print '—' in the p column with the footnote 'within-paper moderator; supported by grouped CV + cluster bootstrap instead'. Honest dashes beat wrong p-values. Always permute only among non-missing papers so the subset is fixed.

[major] (D6 / §8 validation plan — per-moderator permutation p with 10–20 moderators tested, no family-wise control) The design tests every moderator and headlines the max-ΔLL winner, but the permutation p reported for that winner is a per-moderator p. Under a global null with 15 candidates, the expected minimum p is ~0.03–0.07 — the headline 'significant' moderator is exactly what selection produces from noise. Reporting the full ranked table (which D6 does) is disclosure, not correction; it doesn't fix the headline claim's calibration. This is the design's own forking-paths guard failing at the last step.
FAILURE: Demo says 'timing, permutation p = 0.02.' Q&A: 'you tested fifteen moderators; what's the family-wise probability that your best one hits p ≤ 0.02 under the null?' Answer under the design as written: roughly 25%. The pre-registration story collapses in one question.
FIX: Westfall–Young max-T for free: inside each of the 200 permutations you are already looping over all moderators — record the MAX ΔLL across moderators per permutation; family-wise p for the headline = fraction of permutations whose max ΔLL ≥ observed winner's ΔLL. One extra array, zero extra model fits. Report both per-moderator p and this selection-adjusted p in the table, and quote the adjusted one in the demo.

[major] (D7 / Scope item 7 / context §12 Pass 5 — agentic remap 'held-out gain test') The proposal agent is fed 5–10 residual contradiction pairs FROM THE FULL DATASET — i.e., it selects the new moderator after seeing the outcome labels — and the moderator is then evaluated by grouped CV on the SAME papers. That is feature selection outside the CV loop (Ambroise & McLachlan): the reported 'held-out gain +X%' is optimistically biased by construction, and nothing was actually held out from the selection step. The demo narrative (§9) commits to saying 'held-out gain +X%' verbatim.
FAILURE: The agent proposes 'training status' precisely because the contradiction pairs it saw split on it; CV on the same corpus confirms it; the demo claims held-out validation of the agentic loop — the project's differentiating feature. One judge question ('held out from what? did the agent see these papers' outcomes?') converts the flagship agentic claim into an example of the exact selective-exploration failure the Zou paper warns about.
FIX: Cheapest honest fix: split papers 50/50 before Pass 3; the agent sees contradiction pairs only from the discovery half; ΔLL for the new moderator is evaluated only on the validation half (grouped CV within it). With ~50 papers it's noisy — so pre-commit to reporting the sign and CI, not a polished '+X%'. If corpus is too small to split, rename the claim everywhere to 'cross-validated gain (moderator proposed post hoc)' and say the caveat out loud before a judge does.

[major] (D5 + Scope item 3 + Gate G3 — normalized entropy as the headline disagreement number) Three stacked issues. (1) Pseudoreplication: entropy is computed over findings, so one paper contributing 15 same-direction rows deflates (or inflates) the headline 'disagreement' number — D6 guards the CV and bootstrap against clustering but leaves the single most-quoted demo number unweighted. (2) Conditioning on classifiability: excluding mixed/unclear from entropy is MNAR — 'unclear' concentrates in abstract-only papers (paywall-correlated, per research-notes unknown #4) and 'mixed' concentrates in papers where extraction granularity failed, i.e., precisely the heterogeneous papers whose disagreement you claim to measure. The headline entropy is therefore 'entropy among findings we could read and cleanly classify,' presented as 'literature disagreement.' (3) The G3 gate 'probe entropy ≥ 0.4' on a ~10-paper probe: plug-in entropy on N≈10–25 findings has small-sample bias ~(K−1)/2N (~0.05–0.10 normalized) plus large sampling noise — the scale/no-scale decision at 8 PM can flip on a coin.
FAILURE: Demo: 'global disagreement 0.85.' Q&A: 'a quarter of your rows were excluded as unclear and one paper contributes 20% of the remaining rows — what is the paper-level number and how does exclusion correlate with full-text access?' No prepared answer. Separately, Saturday 8 PM: probe entropy reads 0.36 on 12 findings, G3 says kill the best topic — a decision driven by estimator bias, not the literature.
FIX: (1) Make paper-level entropy (each paper's modal direction, or rows weighted 1/findings_per_paper) the co-primary number; show both. (2) Report the mixed/unclear exclusion rate next to every entropy figure, plus a sensitivity entropy treating 'unclear' as a fourth category, and cross-tab unclear-rate × full-text status — three cheap numbers that pre-empt the MNAR question. (3) For G3, compute entropy with Miller–Madow correction and a paper-level bootstrap interval; gate on the interval overlapping 0.4, not the point estimate.

[major] (D6 tree thresholds + Scope item 5 (the 'multiverse map')) The tree — the demo centerpiece — is the least-validated artifact in the plan. (a) 'min_samples_leaf ≥ 5 findings AND ≥ 3 papers': sklearn's DecisionTreeClassifier has no group-aware leaf constraint; the ≥3-papers rule cannot be passed as a parameter, so as written it silently won't exist. (b) Leaf class purities are in-sample; at depth 3 on 50–150 rows they will look far cleaner than reality, and no grouped-CV loss or stability check for the TREE (as opposed to single moderators) is specified — the D6 bootstrap records moderator rank frequency, not split identity. (c) Missingness policy for the multivariate tree is unspecified: complete-case across several moderators shrinks to a tiny biased intersection, while missing-as-category invites a headline branch that is really 'abstract-only papers' (MNAR reporting artifact). (d) Numeric dose must be discretized for the D6 one-hot logistic and for readable tree labels; bins are nowhere pre-registered, and the C-topic story is literally about a ~500 vs 1000 mg cutpoint — choosing bins after seeing outcomes recreates the forking paths the doc claims to have pre-registered away.
FAILURE: The rendered tree shows a dramatic 'dose > 500 mg → negative' leaf with 7 findings; a judge clicks it and finds all 7 rows come from 2 papers (the ≥3-paper rule never executed), the leaf shows 100% purity that a grouped-CV check would have put near chance, and a sibling branch is 'dose = missing → null' — three of the doc's own §41 failure modes (4, 6, and the missingness rule from §19) live on screen simultaneously.
FIX: (a) Fit with sample_weight = 1/findings_per_paper AND run a post-fit leaf audit: any leaf with <3 distinct papers gets merged into its parent (or greyed out and excluded from narration) — 20 lines of code. (b) Report one number: tree grouped-CV log loss vs the same-subset dummy baseline; reuse the existing paper-bootstrap loop to report 'root split = dose in X% of resamples' — split-identity stability falls out for free. (c) Encode missing as an explicit 'not reported' category, render it visually distinct, and adopt a hard demo rule: never narrate a missingness branch. (d) Pin dose bins in the question config at G2 (topic lock), from the literature's own narrative (e.g., <500 / 500–1000 / >1000 mg), before any outcome data is analyzed; let only the tree, not you, pick data-driven cutpoints, and say which is which.

[major] (D6 paper-level bootstrap (500 resamples) interacting with min-support thresholds and grouped CV) Two unspecified choices will be made implicitly at 2 AM and both have a wrong option. (1) Group identity of resampled duplicates: if the two copies of a twice-sampled paper get distinct group IDs, GroupKFold can put identical rows in train and test → leakage → inflated ΔLL inside every resample → inflated 'top-3 in X%' stability claims. (2) Eligibility churn: with ~30 papers, each resample drops ~36% of papers in expectation; moderators near the 3-paper/8-finding thresholds will flicker in and out of eligibility across resamples, so 'top-3 rank frequency' silently mixes 'ranked low' with 'not evaluated'. The denominator convention (all 500 resamples vs. resamples where eligible) changes the headline stability number by tens of percentage points and the design doesn't pick one.
FAILURE: 'Dose ranked top-3 in 87% of resamples' is on the slide. Under the hood: duplicates were relabeled as new groups (leakage pushed every moderator's gain up) and dose was only eligible in 60% of resamples (denominator was eligible-only). The honest number is ~50% of all resamples. Anyone who asks for the definition of the 87% forces a live recomputation you can't do.
FIX: Specify in the doc before coding: resampled copies keep the ORIGINAL paper_id as the CV group (copies always co-fold; no identical-row leakage); support thresholds are checked on distinct original papers; report stability as 'top-3 in a% of all resamples (eligible in b%)' — both numbers, fixed denominator of 500. Also pre-commit the cut-order fallback already in §6: if 500 resamples don't finish, 200 with a fixed seed beats 500 without one.
